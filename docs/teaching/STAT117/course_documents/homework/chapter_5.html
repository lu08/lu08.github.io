Conduct a correlation analysis using the States data set to determine whether various factors are associated with EDUCEXPE, the average educational expenditure per pupil. These factors are STUTEACH, student-teacher ratio; SATV, average verbal SAT score for the state; and TEACHPAY, the average salary of public school teachers in the state.

  1. Create a matrix scatterplot of the variables and determine whether a linear model is an appropriate representation of the relationships between all pairs of these variables.
  2. Calculate the correlation matrix between all pairs of these variables.
  3. Do states in which expenditures per pupil are higher tend to pay their teachers more?
  4. Do states in which expenditures per pupil are higher tend to have higher verbal SAT scores, on average?
  5. Do states in which expenditures per pupil are higher tend to have lower pupil-per-teacher ratios?

The matrix scatterplots suggest that linear models are appropriate for these relationships.   3. In part 1, a positive, yet weak, correlation was noted between taking blood pressure medication and blood pressure level. That is, those who take blood pressure medication have higher blood pressure levels, on average, than those who don’t. What is a likely explanation for this relationship?

Show solution
  1. The correlation between DIABP1 and BPMEDS1 is positive but weak (r = .18). People taking blood-pressure medication have slightly higher diastolic blood pressure on average. Exercise 5.28

Read the following excerpt from the article “TV, video games at night may cause sleep problems in kids”, by Amanda Gardner on June 27, 2011. You do not need to read the entire article to answer the related questions.

Watching television or playing video games close to bedtime can act like a jolt of caffeine to young children, making them more likely to experience difficulty falling asleep, nightmares, and daytime fatigue, a new study in the journal Pediatrics suggests.

In the study, 28% of preschoolers who watched TV or played video games for at least 30 minutes after 7 p.m. had sleep problems most nights of the week, versus 19% of children whose TV and video-game use took place only before 7 p.m.

The study included 612 children ages 3 to 5. Garrison and her colleagues asked the parents to keep a detailed diary of their child’s media consumption over the course of one week, including the title, timing, and duration of each show or game. (To determine the level of violence, researchers consulted parental guidance ratings and actually analyzed specific shows and games.)

On average, the kids spent 73 minutes per day in front of a TV or computer. The more time they spent watching TV or playing video games in the evening, the more likely they were to experience a sleep problem.

  1. According to the results of this study, if you had a much younger brother or sister, should you limit television and video game time after 7 p.m. in order to ensure a good night’s sleep? Explain. Be as detailed as you can.
  2. Create the clustered bar graph depicting the relationship between whether or not the child watched television or played video games for at least 30 minutes after 7 p.m. and whether or not the child had sleep problems most nights of the week as described in the article. Make sure to label your axes and the bars.
  3. Based on the information in the excerpt from the article, is it fair to conclude that the number of children who watch TV after 7 p.m. and have sleep problems is more than the number of children who don’t watch TV after 7 p.m. and have sleep problems? Why or why not?
Show solution
  1. The results do not establish that limiting evening television or video games will ensure good sleep. The variables may be associated without being causally related; a third factor could influence both media use and sleep.
  2. The excerpt gives percentages, not the raw counts needed to recover a unique clustered bar graph of counts. A graph of the reported percentages can show 28% for children using media after 7 p.m. and 19% for those whose use took place only before 7 p.m.
  3. No. The percentages do not reveal the group sizes. If relatively few children watched after 7 p.m., their number with sleep problems could be smaller even though their rate was higher.

Exercise 5.31

What (if anything) is wrong with the following statement about the NELS data? “The correlation between region and the number of Advanced Placement classes offered is r = .11, indicating that the higher the region, the more Advanced Placement courses offered, on average.”

Show solution

Region is a nominal variable with more than two categories and has no inherent order. Therefore, a correlation and an interpretation such as “higher region” are not meaningful. A contingency-table analysis is more appropriate.

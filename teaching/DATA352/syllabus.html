Course Description

Covers techniques for modeling and simulating random systems that are otherwise complicated to study. Topics may include sampling from probability distributions, approximate sampling, Monte Carlo and MCMC methods, bootstrap, and simulation-based inference.

Prerequisite: CSCI 149, CSCI 150, STAT 120, or DATA 505.

Learning Outcomes

Upon completing this course, students will:

  • have a basic understanding of how simulation can be used in various applications;
  • have a basic understanding of probability and statistical concepts;
  • be able to use common parametric distributions to model random observations;
  • be able to perform statistical inference using a few simulation-based techniques;
  • be able to simulate random samples and random processes; and
  • be able to design and implement simple algorithms to perform simulations.

Text and Reading Materials

Topics in this class are relevant to a few different fields in statistics, especially probability and computational statistics.

  • Reading materials will be posted on the course webpage throughout the semester.
  • For probability, we will mostly use Introduction to Probability (Grinstead and Snell, 2012), which is available online.
  • For computational statistics, topics will roughly follow Statistical Computing with R (Rizzo, 2019). This book is not required for this class. It is more in-depth than the scope of this class and requires substantial statistical knowledge.

Grading

Final grades are assigned according to the standard scale: A = 92 to 100; A- = 90 to 92; B+ = 88 to 90; B = 82 to 88; B- = 80 to 82; C+ = 78 to 80; C = 72 to 78; C- = 70 to 72; F = 0 to 70. If registered as an undergraduate: D+ = 68 to 70; D = 62 to 68; D- = 60 to 62; F = 0 to 60.

  • Homework quizzes (20%; two drops allowed): Homework problems and solutions will be assigned roughly weekly. Homework is not collected; instead, a quiz will be given at the beginning of the lecture when the homework is due. Quiz content is directly based on the homework questions. There are no make-ups for this grade under any circumstance; the two lowest grades may be dropped.

Regular attendance and active participation in group assignments and class discussions are expected. There are no make-ups for this grade under any circumstance; the two lowest grades may be dropped.
  • Exams (3 \(\times\) 20%): There are three in-class exams: two midterms and one final. Exams are closed book, notes, and electronics. For each exam, you may use one double-sided 8.5-by-11-inch (or A4) reference sheet.
